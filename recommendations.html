01 · Nuclear / SMRs

Space nuclear power is now the biggest new-money line in the nuclear lane

NASA accounts for $76.1M of the $102.8M in 22 new nuclear awards this window, almost all of it two Glenn Research Center task orders to Sierra Lobo for the Space Reactor-1 "Freedom" Nuclear Power Module power conversion system. The newest award in the whole refresh (Oct 28) is also NASA nuclear-power work. Worth following: further SR-1 task orders under the same vehicle.

02 · Nuclear / SMRs

Nuclear dollars fell about 89% versus the prior 90 days, but that is one $900M award, not a slowdown

Nuclear went from $940.8M (20 awards, May 15 to Aug 13) to $102.8M (22 awards) now. The prior window held DOE's $900M HALEU task order to American Centrifuge Operating (Centrus), already a FedForge flagship. Award counts were flat (20 to 22). Fuel-cycle money arrives in large, irregular blocks, so quarter-over-quarter totals in this lane are noisy. Flagship: HALEU production task order →

03 · Semiconductors / CHIPS

Commerce / NIST is the fastest-accelerating agency: 25 grants vs 1

NIST 60NANB26D grants rose from 1 ($1.5M) in the prior window to 25 ($52.9M) in this one, 20 of them ($41.6M) in semiconductors. They mostly fund equipment, test, packaging and training at regional universities rather than fabs. Department of Commerce overall: 4 awards to 28. Worth following: whether these sites become nodes for later CHIPS R&D work.

04 · Magnets / rare earths

DOE's regional critical-minerals (CORE-CM) grants are about 45% of the magnets lane

Five DOE Office of Fossil Energy and Carbon Management awards of about $7.5M each (Wyoming, Virginia Tech, Illinois, Georgia Tech, UT Austin) total $37.5M, out of $83.7M across 29 magnets / rare-earth awards. Smaller IIJA commercialization grants went to two companies, Loukus Technologies (rare-earth mining value-add) and CorePower Magnetics (rare-earth-free magnets). Worth following: these regional studies are the scouting layer for future feedstock and processing projects.

05 · Nuclear / SMRs

Waste transmutation and state siting are early-stage signals worth tracking

ARPA-E's NEWTON program (Nuclear Energy Waste Transmutation Optimized Now) made three company awards: General Atomics, Transmutex U.S.A. and Omega-P R&D, together $7.2M. Separately, DOE Office of Nuclear Energy gave $500K each to Utah, Tennessee and Louisiana for community outreach and a draft host agreement for "nuclear lifecycle innovation" work. Small dollars, but these awards show which states are engaging on fuel-cycle siting.

06 · Magnets / rare earths

State Department enters the lanes: critical-minerals screening abroad and SMR workshops

The State Department had no awards in these lanes in the prior window and 2 this window ($7.2M). The larger one is a $6.9M cooperative agreement to bolster export controls, customs and investment screening in up to 33 critical-mineral producer countries. The other plans small modular reactor technical workshops. Worth following: diplomacy-side spending often comes before supply-chain deals.

07 · All lanes

Expect a DoD backfill: zero DoD awards and zero verified public tickers so far this window

The prior window had 16 DoD awards ($124.7M), including Raytheon's $48.0M ATSP order and two $12.9M NdFeB magnet-block buys from e-VAC and Noveon. This window has none, and contracts overall fell from 25 to 14. DoD contract records reach USAspending on a delay of about 90 days, and only one award in the refresh is dated after Sep 25. Read this window as incomplete. DoD magnet and chip awards from Aug to Nov should appear in later refreshes, and that is where public-company primes (Raytheon, GlobalFoundries, IBM, Microchip) showed up last time.
